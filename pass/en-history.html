History

Two centuries on the Utrata

From the summer manor of abbots to a residence reborn — the story of Pass runs through ten chapters, joined by a single thread: this place has never stopped being loved.

Chapter I 13th–18th c. The abbots of Czerwińsk

A summer seat of abbots

From the late thirteenth century the Pass estate belonged to the monastery of the Canons Regular at Czerwińsk, and in the eighteenth century served as the summer residence of its abbots. An inventory of 1788 describes the “old manor at Pass”: a single-storey timber house with a shingled roof, its state chamber converted from a former chapel — wallpapered rooms, wooden floors, masonry fireplaces and tiled stoves. The manor has not survived; it may have stood exactly where the palace stands today.

Chapter II 1807 Napoleon at Błonie

The emperor, a legend and a belt

The best-known legend about the name “Pass” has Napoleon leaving his military belt (pas) here as he passed through. There is a grain of truth in it: Napoleonic troops were indeed quartered in the area, and the emperor himself stopped at an inn in Błonie — where, on 1 January 1807, he first set eyes on Maria Walewska, who would become his celebrated mistress.

Chapter III 1830–1835 Jan Henryk Geysmer

The palace is born

In 1830 the estate was bought by Jan Henryk Geysmer, a Hamburg-born Warsaw industrialist. Under him the classicist palace was raised — to a design attributed on strong stylistic grounds to Chrystian Piotr Aigner. Geysmer died only five years later, aged 55; his nephews Jan and Ernest were close friends of Frédéric Chopin.

Chapter IV 1837–1851 Ludwik & Borys Halpert

A banker’s house, an actress’s stage

In 1837 the palace passed at auction to the Warsaw banker Ludwik Halpert, later ennobled in the Kingdom of Poland. In the 1840s the architect Adolf Schuch remodelled the interiors and fitted the west wing with a private theatre — a stage between two Corinthian columns. It was built with the host’s sister-in-law in mind: Leontyna Halpertowa, wife of Borys Halpert, director of the Warsaw Theatres.

Chapter V 1803–1895 Leontyna Halpertowa

The first actress of Warsaw

Leontyna Halpertowa was held to be the greatest actress of her age — Racine’s Phèdre and Hermione, Joan of Arc, Klara in the première of Fredro’s “Maiden Vows”. At Pass, far from the city, she rehearsed her roles at leisure. When she died in her ninety-second year, the Warsaw theatres cancelled their rehearsals on the day of her funeral.

Chapter VI 1851–1877 Count Kazimierz Plater-Zyberk

A quarter-century under a count

In 1851 the estate — some 1,500 morgen — was acquired by Count Kazimierz Plater-Zyberk, a veteran of the November Uprising who had paid for it with exile and years of service in the Caucasus. His daughter Cecylia Plater-Zyberk, the celebrated educator and social reformer, was born on the Pass estate. In 1877 his son sold the property back to the Halperts.

Chapter VII 1891–1930 Anna & Luiza Halpert

The sisters, and Beatrix’s diary

After 1891 the estate passed to the sisters Anna and Luiza Halpert, who ran it as a model farm. In August 1910 their English sister-in-law Beatrix recorded a family visit in her diary: “Pass and the gardens are charming… the best linen sheets I have ever seen, almost like satin.” After damage by Russian troops in 1914–15, Anna restored the palace and replanted the park. She died in 1930.

Chapter VIII 1932–1944 Ziemowit Jasiński

The last private owner

Ziemowit Jasiński invested above all in the estate: a full brick wall, a repaired roof, plumbing, and a rose parterre with a stone sundial before the palace. The house survived the war largely unscathed. Jasiński never stopped dreaming of recovering Pass; he visited for the last time in 2004, approved of the restoration then beginning, and died the following year.

Chapter IX 1946–2003 State ownership

Decades of neglect

Nationalised in 1946, the palace became a plant-breeding station, then in the 1980s a party academy, and finally stood abandoned, falling into ruin. In the 1990s Bank Pekao S.A. documented the building meticulously and repaired the roof — but went no further.

Chapter X 2004–today The Warter Group

Rebirth

In 2004 the palace was acquired by the Warter Group, which undertook its complete restoration — finished in 2011 — joining classicist principles with modern engineering. The work continues in the park, an outstanding piece of landscape art restored in partnership with the environmental office of the town of Błonie. Patience, faith and one man’s dream have made Pałac Pass astonish once again.
