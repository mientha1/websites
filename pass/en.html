The Estate

A house that remembers two centuries

The palace at Pass was raised in 1830 for the Warsaw industrialist Jan Henryk Geysmer, to a design attributed to Chrystian Piotr Aigner, one of the masters of Polish classicism. Over two centuries it has been the home of bankers and counts, the private rehearsal stage of the most celebrated actress of her age — and it has survived wars and decades of neglect.

Meticulously restored in 2011, it stands once more among the finest residences near Warsaw: a setting for celebrations, meetings and film work, yet above all what it has always been — a house with a history, in the quiet of an old park.

The Palace

Marble, crystal and light

Behind the classicist façade: marble floors, crystal chandeliers, mirrors and antique furniture. In the 1840s Ludwik Halpert fitted the west wing with a private theatre — a stage set between two Corinthian columns, where Leontyna Halpertowa, the leading actress of Warsaw, rehearsed her roles.

History

From an abbots’ manor to the Halpert home

The Pass estate once belonged to the abbots of Czerwińsk, quartered Napoleon’s troops, and for most of the nineteenth century was home to the Halpert family. Every owner left a trace here — and every trace can still be found.

The Park

Seventeen hectares of silence

The English landscape park on the Utrata ranks among the most precious gardens of the so-called Kampinos group — in its variety it surpasses them all. Ponds, ancient trees, white gazebos, and a black orangery among the greenery. Its patient restoration has been under way for years.
