The Palace

A residence on the Utrata

A classicist palace raised in 1830 and restored in 2011 — its architecture attributed to Chrystian Piotr Aigner.

Architecture

The hand of a master

No document names the palace’s architect, but the evidence points to Chrystian Piotr Aigner — builder of St Alexander’s Church in Warsaw and of the palaces at Przeworsk, Olesin and Zarzecze. Pass corresponds closely with his mature classicism: calm symmetry, Italian proportion, and ornament used sparingly.

The commission came from Jan Henryk Geysmer, a Hamburg-born Warsaw industrialist and one of the first figures of the city’s young industrial elite. The palace rose on ground where a wooden manor of the abbots of Czerwińsk had stood for centuries.

Interiors

Rooms that repay attention

Marble floors, crystal chandeliers, richly worked doors, mirrors, antique furniture, paintings and sculpture — the rooms at Pass reward anyone with an eye for craft. For all their formality they remain surprisingly domestic: this is still a residence, not a museum.

In the 1840s the architect Adolf Schuch remodelled the interiors for Ludwik Halpert: a new entrance to the ballroom, a new staircase, and — in the west wing — a private theatre with a stage between two Corinthian columns, built for Leontyna Halpertowa, the finest actress of the Warsaw stage.

Restoration

A return to splendour

After decades of post-war decline the palace came back to life in 2004, when the Warter Group took it into its care. Seven years of painstaking restoration, completed in 2011, returned the residence to its former glory and to the map of Mazovia’s classicist monuments.

The restored ensemble joins classicist principles with modern engineering: the historic fabric untouched, the comfort entirely of today.
