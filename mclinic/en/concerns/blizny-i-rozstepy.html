Your concern

Scars and stretch marks

Scars and stretch marks form when the skin's structure is damaged or overstretched. A series of well-chosen treatments can soften and even them out.

Understanding the concern

Scars form after injury to the skin; their look depends on the extent of damage and how the wound heals. Keloids and hypertrophic scars result from disturbed collagen synthesis and can cause pain, burning or itching. Post-acne scars affect up to 80–90% of acne patients and are classified as hypertrophic, atrophic (ice-pick, boxcar, rolling, shallow) or discolored. Stretch marks are parallel, spindle-shaped bands of thinned skin — initially red and inflammatory, later pale — caused by overstretching or weakening of the collagen and elastin network, typically on the thighs, buttocks, abdomen and breasts.

Stretch mark therapy is similar to scar therapy and requires a series of treatments, with satisfying effectiveness and lasting results. The method is chosen by a doctor or cosmetologist at a consultation, depending on the location and size of the lesions.

Possible approaches

These are possible directions, not a prescription. The choice of method always follows a consultation and examination.

Fractional CO2 laser The fractional CO2 laser corrects post-acne, post-traumatic and surgical scars as well as stretch marks. → Secret RF DUO microneedle radiofrequency Secret RF combines microneedle radiofrequency with a laser to reduce atrophic scars and stretch marks. → Dermapen 4® microneedling Dermapen 4 microneedling softens post-acne, surgical and burn scars as well as stretch marks. → Erbium-glass laser The non-ablative erbium-glass laser improves the look of scars and stretch marks with short downtime. → Lavieen thulium laser The Lavieen thulium laser is effective for treating all kinds of scars and stretch marks. → Icoone® Icoone vacuum massage supports scar reduction and skin regeneration. →
